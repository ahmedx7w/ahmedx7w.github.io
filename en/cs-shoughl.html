Shoughl: remote work that feels permanent

A GCC remote-jobs marketplace where employers hire full-time, pre-vetted people and candidates find stable roles with real UAE companies. One platform, two audiences with opposite questions, in Arabic and English.

Platform
Responsive web
Languages
Arabic & English
My role
UI/UX designer
2Audiences, each with its own landing page and journey
3Steps from sign-up to hired, each with a time promise
AR ⇄ ENOne layout system for both reading directions

The challenge

Remote hiring only works if both sides believe it’s real. The site has to earn that belief from two very different visitors.

  • Remote sounds temporary

    Remote work is often read as freelance gigs. Candidates want a monthly salary they can count on; employers want people who stay.

  • Two audiences, opposite questions

    Employers ask what a hire costs and whether the person is vetted. Candidates ask whether the company is real and how they get paid.

  • Two reading directions

    Arabic and English speakers share one product, so every layout has to work right to left and left to right without a second design.

Design strategy

Business goal
Become the place GCC companies go to hire full-time remote staff, and where professionals find stable remote roles.
Who it’s for
Employers and hiring managers across the GCC, and professionals who want a full-time remote job with a UAE company.
Core tasks
Employers: estimate the saving, browse talent, post a job. Candidates: build a profile, get verified, apply.
Success means
An employer sees the cost case on one screen, and a candidate goes from sign-up to a verified profile within a day.

Two front doors

A two-way switch sits at the top of every page: “Find the right talent” or “I am the talent”. Each side gets its own headline, its own proof and its own main button, so nobody reads the other side’s pitch to find theirs.

For employers

Hire remote employees, not freelancers

Hire Talent Remotely
  1. See what you’d savePick a country and a local salary; the page shows the monthly and yearly difference.
  2. Browse talentPre-vetted, full-time people, presented as employees who stay.
  3. Post a jobPublish the role and receive applications on the platform.
  4. Buy creditsPricing runs on credits, bought when you’re ready to hire.
For candidates

Build a stable remote career

Get Hired Remotely
  1. Build your free profileExperience, skills and CV, in under 10 minutes.
  2. Get verifiedWithin 10 to 14 hours, so companies trust the profile faster.
  3. Apply and get hiredShortlist, interview and start, all from home.

Answering the money question first

An employer’s first objection is cost, so the employer page answers it before anything else. Pick a country, set a local salary, and the local hire and the remote hire appear side by side, line by line: salary, insurance, office, admin and visa.

The saving is shown three ways, per month, per year and as a percentage, because the person approving a hire and the person running the team think in different units. Then one button: start hiring.

Designed in both directions

The layout is built on start and end edges rather than left and right, so the Arabic version mirrors as a whole instead of being redrawn. A few things never mirror.

Mirrors

  • Navigation order and the employer and candidate switch
  • Step sequences, which read from right to left
  • Arrows and other icons that point somewhere
  • Sliders, which fill from the right

Never mirrors

  • Numbers, salaries and percentages
  • The logo
  • Phone numbers, emails and web addresses
  • Icons with no direction, such as a checkmark

What I’d measure next

The site is live, but I don’t have outcome numbers to share yet. These are the three I’d watch first.

  • Calculator to hiringHow many employers who move the salary slider go on to browse talent or post a job.
  • Profile completionHow many candidates finish their profile in one sitting, and where the rest stop.
  • Verification timeWhether the 10 to 14 hour promise holds as sign-ups grow.
