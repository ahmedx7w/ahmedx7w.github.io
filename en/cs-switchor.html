Switchor: from commodity to brand

A product website for Switchor, a brand of brazed tungsten carbide tips. Most carbide is sold loose and unbranded, so buyers compare on price alone. The site had to show why one grade, checked lot by lot and sealed in a smart 1 kg box, is worth choosing, for workshops and for the distributors who stock it.

The challenge

Carbide tips look the same in every box. Before anyone pays more for one brand, they need to see what they are actually buying.

  • Carbide is a commodity

    Loose, unbranded tips look identical, so price is usually the only thing buyers compare.

  • The real cost is hidden

    A cracked tip costs little, but it can ruin a casting worth hundreds and an afternoon of machine time.

  • Three different buyers

    A workshop owner, a tool distributor and an OEM buyer each need a different reason to trust the same product.

Design strategy

Business goal
Position Switchor as the trusted, shelf-ready carbide brand, and turn visits into Discovery Box orders and distributor enquiries.
Who it’s for
Machine workshops, industrial tool distributors and OEM buyers, in the UAE and beyond.
Core tasks
Understand the grade, pick a profile, then order a 5 kg Discovery Box or apply to distribute.
Success means
A buyer understands why Switchor costs what it does before they ask for a price.

The box is the product

Switchor sells the same carbide others sell loose, so the packaging carries the difference. The site puts the 1 kg box at the centre: a 3D model you can rotate 360° and click to inspect, with four features that matter on a real shelf.

The most useful one is the tear-off stub. It carries the model, grade, weight and a Code 128 barcode, so a workshop pins it to its stock board and reorders with one scan.

  • Sealed 1 kg, barcode on the boxReady for shelves and ERP systems without relabelling.
  • Tear-off reorder stubRestocking takes one scan instead of a search through old invoices.
  • Safety printed on the boxEuropean GHS08 hazard marking, so distributors never relabel.
  • One grade, masteredYG6 only, checked for hardness and density on every lot.

Three buyers, three doors

A selector near the top asks who you are, then shows the reasons and tools that fit that buyer.

I run a workshop

Consistent cuts, no surprise cracks.

  • A speed advisor and a crack-free brazing guide
  • A 5 kg Discovery Box to test on your own machines
  • A calculator for your monthly use

I distribute tools

Shelves that sell and repeat orders.

  • Barcoded, ERP-ready cartons
  • A reorder stub that works as a silent salesperson
  • A downloadable ERP file and a partner application

Custom or OEM supply

Bespoke shapes at volume.

  • Custom sintered shapes
  • Volume supply from the sintering source
  • A roadmap of upcoming grades

The business case in five seconds

Instead of competing with cheaper tips, the site prices Switchor against the cost of failure. A slider sets how many boxes a workshop uses each month, and the page shows the profit protected per year, the failures avoided and the machine time won back, in AED or USD.

It answers whether the price is worth it before the buyer reaches the contact page.

What I’d measure next

The site is live, but I don’t have outcome numbers to share yet. These are the three I’d watch first.

  • Discovery Box ordersHow many visitors who use the calculator or the 3D box go on to order the 5 kg box.
  • Distributor applicationsHow many partner applications start from the reorder-stub story or the ERP file.
  • Which door convertsWhether workshops, distributors or OEM buyers enquire most, to decide where new content goes.
