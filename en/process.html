How I work

A clear path from the first question to the final file, so you always know where we are and what comes next.

Details first

Small details make the big difference: spacing, weight, and what a button does after you press it.

Function comes first

Aesthetics aren't everything. Every element has to earn its place by what it does for the user.

Both directions

Arabic and English are designed together from the start, not translated at the end.

Four phases

We don't move on until we agree on the phase before.

  1. Listen & understand

    We start with a conversation about your project, its goals and its audience. I ask a lot, because the right question early saves weeks later.

    Your part

    Share what you have: examples you like or dislike, and any existing brand files.

    What you get

    • A written brief we both agree on
    • Scope and deliverables
    • A first timeline

  2. Research & plan

    I study competitors and your audience, and decide what the design needs to say before deciding how it looks.

    Your part

    Review the direction and tell me if it feels like your brand.

    What you get

    • Research summary
    • Visual direction or moodboard
    • User flows for interfaces

  3. Design & refine

    I turn the direction into designs and prototypes, in Arabic and English together, then we refine them round by round.

    Your part

    Honest feedback. “I don't like it” with a reason is worth more than “looks nice”.

    What you get

    • Logo concepts or screen designs
    • An interactive prototype
    • Structured review rounds

  4. Deliver & support

    You get everything you need to use the work straight away, and I stay available for support and changes after launch.

    Your part

    Tell me how the design performs in the real world, so we can improve it.

    What you get

    • Organised final files
    • Brand guidelines or Figma files
    • A clear hand-off for developers

Designing in two languagesisn't translation. It's designing twice, with the same care.

  • Balanced type

    Arabic reads smaller at the same size, so I balance sizes and line heights until both languages feel equal.

  • Correct mirroring

    Arrows, layout order and progress bars mirror. Numbers, logos and play buttons don't.

  • Real copy

    I design with real copy in both languages, because an Arabic sentence can be much shorter or longer than its English version.

  • Smooth switching

    Switching language should keep people where they are, not send them back to the homepage.

Questions

If your question isn't here, send it with your brief.

Do you design in both Arabic and English?

Yes, from the first sketch. I pair Arabic and Latin typefaces that balance, build layouts that mirror correctly, and test real copy lengths in both languages so nothing breaks after translation.

How long does a project take?

It depends on scope. For reference, the SpotSwift app took five weeks and the learning platform about six months. After our first conversation I'll give you a timeline for your project.

What do you need from me to start?

A short description of the project and its audience, examples you like or dislike, any existing brand files, and a rough idea of budget and timing. The brief on the Hire me page covers it in four steps.

What will I receive at the end?

Everything you need to use the work. For identities: logo files in vector and image formats plus brand guidelines. For interfaces: organised Figma files, an interactive prototype and exported assets.

How do feedback and revisions work?

We agree on each phase before moving to the next. You review the designs at set points, and I refine them based on your feedback.

Can you work with our developers?

Yes. I hand over organised files with clear specs for spacing, colour and states, and stay available to answer their questions during the build.

Which tools do you use?

Figma for interfaces and prototypes, Adobe Illustrator for logos and vector work, and Adobe Photoshop for images.
