Chaudary Anwar Group: six businesses, one front door

A corporate website for a Sharjah holding group whose businesses range from buying dead stock and clearing industrial lots to recruitment and fine art. It had to explain an unusual group in one scroll, and get a seller’s stock offer to the right desk.

Type
Corporate website
Format
Single page, responsive
My role
UI design and front-end development
6Businesses presented with one shared pattern
7Numbered chapters, from overview to registry
1Form that routes every enquiry to the right business

The challenge

A holding group is easy to describe and hard to picture. Before a company hands over stock details, it needs to understand who it’s dealing with.

  • A group that’s hard to picture

    Dead stock, wholesale trading, industrial salvage, a second industrial branch, recruitment and fine art don’t obviously belong together.

  • Sellers who need certainty

    A company clearing dormant stock wants proof of a real, operating business that pays before it shares inventory details.

  • Many visitors, one site

    Sellers, wholesale buyers, job seekers and art clients arrive at the same address, and each enquiry has to reach the right business.

Design strategy

Business goal
Present the group as one credible operation, and turn visits into stock offers and enquiries.
Who it’s for
Companies clearing dead stock or industrial surplus, wholesale buyers across the GCC, and clients of the group’s other businesses.
Core tasks
Understand the group, find the right business, and send a lot manifest or an enquiry.
Success means
A seller can submit a manifest to the right business from any chapter, without hunting for a contact.

One scroll, seven chapters

Instead of a page per business, the site is a single scroll in numbered chapters. Each one answers the next question a cautious seller asks, in the order they ask it.

  1. 01OverviewWhat is this group?
  2. 02VerticalsWhat does each business do?
  3. 03BlueprintIs the operation real and physical?
  4. 04LeadershipWho runs it?
  5. 05PartnersWho does it work with?
  6. 06InquireHow do I start?
  7. 07RegistryHow is it registered?

Submit Lot ManifestThe main action stays in the header, one click away from every chapter.

Proof that the operation is physical

Trust comes from showing the business exists in the real world. A status bar runs a live Gulf Standard Time clock next to the plus code of the Sharjah headquarters, and a GCC logistics map shows the corridors the group ships through.

An interactive blueprint of the Industrial Area 3 complex lets visitors select each area, from the command office and the dead-stock vault to the industrial yard, the freight fleet and the Artify studio, and see what happens there.

A form that routes itself

One lot-manifest form serves every business. The visitor picks the business, describes the assets and where they are, and the confirmation says exactly what happens next.

What I’d measure next

The site is live, but I don’t have outcome numbers to share yet. These are the three I’d watch first.

  • Manifests by businessWhich businesses the site actually feeds, and where to put effort next.
  • Chapter reached before submittingWhich chapters do the convincing, and which ones visitors skip.
  • Form drop-off by fieldWhether any field, such as quantities, stops people from sending.
